# Introduction

There are two trim approaches documented here:
1. A ["by the book" approach](#step-2-trim-option-a-by-the-book) that strictly follows the service manual
2. A [quick](#step-2-trim-option-b-quick-method) approach that takes less time 
See bowow's video guide on the quick approach: [https://youtu.be/cLIaA3FKEos](https://youtu.be/cLIaA3FKEos).

Necessary tools:

1. 10mm socket or adjustable wrench for 12V battery (not included)  
2. Trim removal tools (included if selected)  
3. Phillips \#2 allen key (included if selected) or similar

Kit components:

1. Head unit harness  
2. WiCAN  
3. 70mm OBD mount bracket  
4. 70mm double-sided tape (two pieces included for redundancy)  
5. 4 zip ties

# Step 2: Trim: Option B: Quick method   

See bowow's video guide on the quick approach: [https://youtu.be/cLIaA3FKEos](https://youtu.be/cLIaA3FKEos).

## Remove the front console covers

1. Remove trim clips on both sides with a forked trim removal tool.
  ![](photos/front_console_removal.jpg)
2. Pry on the top of the front with a flat trim removal tool.
3. Set the front console cover to the side. \
  ![](photos/USB_trim_removed_harness_down.jpg)

## (Optional) Loosen crash pad center
1. Remove screw connecting crash pad lower and crash pad center, circled in green below. \
  ![](photos/USB_trim_removed_harness_down_screw_circled.jpg)

## Install harness
1. Remove original female connector (grey, leftmost) from head unit.
  ![](photos/head_unit_looking_up.jpg)    
2. Connect the male side harness (the side with visible pins) to the original female connector and the head unit.
3. Connect the female side (no visible pins) into the head unit. You should hear clicks in both cases.    
  ![](photos/harness_installed_looking_up.jpg)
4. Pull the OBD end down.

## Mount the OBD bracket

1. Apply the included tape to the OBD bracket.

### Option 1: On outside of crash pad lower
This option is not recommended. a) It is very obvious, and b) the tape may sag in heat due to the torque from the WiCAN.  
In this option, stick the OBD bracket to the crash pad lower trim. Run the harness up and insert the OBD into the bracket. \
  ![](photos/image41.jpg)  

### Option 2: Inside front console cover
This option is easiest if you've already removed the front console cover as part of the quick install approach. This mount location keeps the kit very well hidden, but the WiCAN is less accessible. There are two good mount points within this approach:
### Mount Option 2A
Attach the OBD mount bracket to the plastic ventilation duct. 
  ![](photos/OBD_mount_1.jpg)
### Mount Option 2B
Attach the OBD mount bracket to the inner surface of the steel beam.  
  ![](photos/OBD_mount_2.jpg)
  ![](photos/wican_peeking.jpg)

Mount 2B allows you to remove the WiCAN without removing the front console cover. You shouldn't have to remove the WiCAN much, but in the rare case of issues, or if you want to fully disable the kit behavior in advance of a dealership visit, access can be useful.

## Reassembly

Follow the trim removal instructions in reverse to reassemble everything.

# Step 3: Clean Up

Reconnect the 12 V battery negative terminal. The WiCAN should immediately show a blue light.

## WiCAN setup

The WiCAN by default provides a WiFi access point. Look for a network named `WiCAN_xxxxxxx`, where `xxxxxxx` is the device ID of your unit, and connect to it with the default password `@meatpi#`. From a phone, the network can take a few seconds to appear, and your phone will warn you that it has no internet access; stay connected anyway. Then open [http://192.168.80.1/](http://192.168.80.1/) in a browser to reach the configuration interface. (A WiCAN Pro, not officially supported, uses [http://192.168.0.10/](http://192.168.0.10/) instead.)

In the "Settings" tab, change the access point password. With the default password, anyone within WiFi range of your car who knows the default password could unlock your car (yes, this feature is coming!). If you leave the WiCAN in access point mode, we also suggest disabling BLE for now.

Optionally, the WiCAN can be configured in station mode join your home WiFi so that you can reach it from inside the house. In the "Settings" tab, set "Mode" to "AP+Station", enter your network's "SSID" and "Password", and press "Submit changes". The WiCAN keeps broadcasting its own access point, so you cannot lock yourself out this way. However, access point mode in "AP+Station" can be a bit finicky, so we recommend AP only unless you're using Home Assistant or similar to grab data from your car. The "Status" tab then shows the "Station IP" your router assigned, which is the address to use from your home network.

The full WiCAN WiFi documentation is at [https://meatpihq.github.io/wican-fw/config/wifi](https://meatpihq.github.io/wican-fw/config/wifi).
